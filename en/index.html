Executive Profile

Profile

Adem Filali is an agricultural engineer specializing in animal and forage production and biosurveillance, trained at the École Supérieure d'Agriculture de Mateur (Université de Carthage), where he graduated with Highest Honours in June 2026. His work spans hands-on livestock production systems and international advisory roles in animal health and agritech adoption — including biosurveillance and outbreak-preparedness support with Global Outbreak Solutions, and sustainable-agriculture strategy with GIDI Clean Technology. He combines field experience in dairy and livestock husbandry with data-driven analysis (SAS, R, Python), reflected in his engineering thesis and in a co-authored vector-borne disease study published on medRxiv with a University of Georgia research team. Fluent in Arabic, French, and English, with working Italian, he brings a multilingual, cross-border approach to agricultural and animal-health advisory across Africa and internationally.

Selected Projects

Applied work, from field trial to funding proposal

01 / FIELD RESEARCH

Milk-Feeding Strategies in Pre-Weaned Calves
Engineering Capstone Project

Challenge Determine which liquid feeding strategy best supports growth, feeding behaviour, and resilience to Bovine Respiratory Disease Complex in pre-weaned Montbéliarde calves under intensive farming conditions.
Approach Designed and ran a comparative field trial (whole milk vs. two commercial milk replacers) at Groupe Alfa's SMVDA Errouki farm, applying rigorous statistical modelling to production and health data.
Expertise Applied Animal production trial design; statistical analysis in SAS, R, and Python (PROC GLM, PROC MIXED, ANCOVA, logistic regression).
Outcome Whole-milk-fed calves showed significantly higher average daily gain (P = 0.03–0.04) than milk-replacer groups, supporting feeding management as a first-line biosecurity measure. Thesis defended with Highest Honours before an academic jury at the University of Carthage.
02 / DATA RESEARCH

Climate Change × Agricultural Systems
Independent Research

Challenge Understand how decades of climate variability have shaped agricultural outcomes and food-security risk.
Approach Independent descriptive-analysis project using R and Python on two datasets spanning 11,000 observations and 34 years of agricultural and climate data.
Expertise Applied Data analysis and reproducible research (R/Python), agro-climatic modelling.
Outcome Identified drought exposure as a key driver of food-security risk; reproducible code published on Kaggle.
